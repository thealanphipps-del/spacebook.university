## Attention, Governance, Knowledge & Distributed Intelligence Research Program

**Status:** Public Experimental Charter

---

## 🏛️ Executive Statement

Spacebook.University is an open research initiative exploring whether a privacy-preserving, consent-based, participant-owned civilization substrate can emerge from the interaction of:

* Human attention
* Distributed computation
* Knowledge preservation
* Trust-based governance
* Voluntary economic coordination

The project is experimental.

No claim is made that the proposed architectures, protocols, scoring systems, governance mechanisms, or economic models will function as expected at scale.

The purpose of this experiment is to investigate these questions transparently, document the results publicly, and invite rigorous criticism and collaboration.

---

## 1. Mission

The mission of Spacebook.University is:

> **To investigate whether a civilization-scale network can be built where knowledge, trust, computation, governance, and economic coordination emerge from voluntary participation rather than surveillance, coercion, or concentrated ownership.**

---

## 2. Research Question

The central research question is:

$$\mathbf{Can\ human\ attention\ be\ transformed\ into\ a\ sustainable\ source\ of\ knowledge\ production,\ distributed\ intelligence,\ and\ civilizational\ coordination\ without\ sacrificing\ individual\ sovereignty?}$$

---

## 3. Experimental Domains

The experiment consists of six research domains.

### Domain I: Attention Science
* **Research Questions:**
  * How is attention allocated?
  * What attracts sustained learning?
  * What drives exploration?
  * How do communities form and evolve?
* **Hypothesis:**
  $$\text{Voluntary curiosity outperforms imposed engagement.}$$

### Domain II: Knowledge Preservation
* **Research Questions:**
  * Can operational wisdom survive generations of participants?
  * Can failures become permanent institutional memory?
  * Can doctrine improve outcomes?
* **Hypothesis:**
  $$\text{Civilizations that preserve reasoning outperform civilizations that preserve only information.}$$

### Domain III: Distributed Intelligence
* **Research Questions:**
  * Can consumer devices contribute useful computation?
  * Can federated intelligence emerge from edge devices?
  * Can local inference outperform centralized collection?
* **Hypothesis:**
  $$\text{Distributed intelligence can be coordinated without centralized ownership of data.}$$

### Domain IV: Governance
* **Research Questions:**
  * Can governance emerge from contribution instead of wealth?
  * Can competency improve institutional decisions?
  * Can trust be measured?
* **Hypothesis:**
  $$\text{Contribution-based governance is more resilient than capital-weighted governance.}$$

### Domain V: Economic Coordination
* **Research Questions:**
  * Can attention become productive?
  * Can local businesses benefit from mission-driven engagement?
  * Can participation generate sustainable revenue?
* **Hypothesis:**
  $$\text{Coordinated attention can produce measurable economic value.}$$

### Domain VI: Civilizational Continuity
* **Research Questions:**
  * Can institutions survive founder turnover?
  * Can doctrine survive steward turnover?
  * Can governance survive scale?
* **Hypothesis:**
  $$\text{Knowledge continuity is a prerequisite for long-term institutional survival.}$$

## 4. Core Experimental Systems

The experiment currently investigates the interaction of:

| Layer | Component | Implementation / Role |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Reality Layer** | People, Places, Businesses, Markets, Events | Physical world ground truth; non-simulated sensory events |
| **Facts Layer** | Valkey Substrate | Authoritative, low-latency microsecond event ledger |
| **Relationship Layer** | Neo4j HomeTree | Graph memory and causal topology across entities and invariants |
| **Interpretation Layer** | Spacebook 5D | Visualization, manifold geometry, and exploration navigation |
| **Ontology Layer** | HomeTree | Grounding abstract systems in real-world entities |
| **Governance Layer** | ATTN, rATTN, gATTN | Strict constitutional separation of Economics, Merit, and Trust |

## 5. Participant Rights

Every participant retains:

1. **Right to informed consent**
2. **Right to disconnect**
3. **Right to export data**
4. **Right to revoke participation**
5. **Right to transparency**
6. **Right to inspect algorithms**
7. **Right to challenge governance decisions**

*Participation is strictly voluntary.*

---

## 6. Privacy Commitments

Spacebook.University explicitly rejects surveillance capitalism.

The experiment shall not be designed around:
* Secret data extraction
* Hidden profiling
* Behavioral manipulation
* Dark patterns
* Forced engagement

The project adopts the fundamental principle:
> **"Data belongs to the participant."**

Where possible:
$$\mathbf{Compute\ moves\ to\ data\quad (\text{rather than data moving to compute}).}$$

---

## 7. Governance Commitments

The experiment adopts the following foundational tripartite capital model:

* **Economic Capital (ATTN):** Measures liquid economic value and marketplace utility.
* **Reputation Capital (rATTN):** Measures demonstrated merit, epistemic output, and research.
* **Trust Capital (gATTN):** Measures soulbound governance authority and constitutional stewardship.

### Constitutional Principle:
$$\mathbf{ATTN \mathrel{\kern-0.2em\not\kern-0.3em\Longrightarrow} gATTN}$$

Influence must arise exclusively from:
$$\text{Contribution} + \text{Competence} + \text{Stewardship} + \text{Time}$$

## 8. Success Criteria

The experiment will be considered successful if it demonstrates measurable evidence that:

* **Knowledge:** Participants create, refine, and preserve institutional knowledge.
* **Governance:** Trust-based governance remains functional without plutocratic capture.
* **Privacy:** Useful social and physical coordination occurs with zero surveillance.
* **Compute:** Distributed edge computation provides measurable utility.
* **Economics:** The system generates sustainable revenue without relying on speculative token value.
* **Continuity:** Knowledge survives turnover of original founders and stewards.

---

## 9. Failure Criteria

The experiment shall be considered unsuccessful if:

* **Governance Capture:** Economic capital directly or indirectly acquires governance authority.
* **Privacy Failure:** Participants lose meaningful cryptographic control over their own data.
* **Knowledge Decay:** Operational doctrine becomes fragmented, mythologized, or unusable.
* **Sybil Capture:** Artificial identities obtain disproportionate governance influence.
* **Economic Dependence:** The system survives only through speculative trading loops.
* **Epistemic Drift:** The graph no longer reflects physical reality.

---

## 10. Open Questions

The experiment explicitly acknowledges unresolved challenges:

* How should trust be measured without introducing bias?
* Can civilizational contribution be objectively quantified?
* What are the physical and mathematical limits of distributed edge governance?
* At what rate should participant reputation decay across epochs?
* How can collective human attention be coordinated ethically?
* What constitutional protections are required when scaling to millions of nodes?
* Can multi-regional federation occur without centralizing tendency?

*These questions remain open to investigation.*

## 12. Final Principle

Spacebook.University is not attempting to prove that a particular technology, token, algorithm, or governance mechanism is correct.

$$\mathbf{Spacebook.University\ is\ attempting\ to\ discover\ whether\ a\ civilization\ can\ coordinate\ knowledge,\ trust,\ computation,\ economics,\ and\ governance\ while\ preserving\ individual\ sovereignty.}$$

*The experiment remains open, transparent, and subject to revision in response to evidence.*
